Overview
The product

AI-supported clinical debriefing

DeepBrief analyzes medical simulations and helps trainees and instructors review performance through criteria, transcripts, video, and AI-generated insights.

The challenge

Valuable information, disconnected experience

The previous interface contained useful feedback, but users had to move between separate report, timeline, and transcript views to understand the full story.

My contribution

A connected review workspace

Working directly with CTO Yoad Cohen, I redesigned the core Simulation Overview and iterated through rapid prototypes, feedback, and interface testing.

The Starting Point

The information existed, but the story was fragmented.

Performance outcomes were split into separate columns, categories repeated across the page, and the transcript lived in a long standalone view. Users could access the information, but connecting feedback to the exact moment that produced it required extra navigation and memory.

Design Question

How might we help users move from simulation playback to clear, evidence-based feedback without losing context?

User Flow

A journey built around the moments users were losing.

The redesign connects each major pain point to a specific interface response, moving from session review to evidence, understanding, and continued practice.

01Orient to the session
Pain point

Video, summary, and session details competed for limited space.

Design response

Make the recording the anchor and keep essential context nearby.

Outcome: faster orientation
02Find the right moment
Pain point

The transcript had no search and required scanning a long standalone page.

Design response

Add search, speaker colors, timestamps, and a persistent timeline.

Outcome: less manual scanning
03Understand performance
Pain point

Clinical categories were fragmented across status-based columns.

Design response

Group criteria by clinical category and reveal status within context.

Outcome: clearer hierarchy
04Verify the result
Pain point

Feedback was separated from the transcript and relevant video moment.

Design response

Place written feedback, transcript evidence, and playback together.

Outcome: stronger trust
05Act and return
Pain point

A single-session view did not clearly support long-term engagement.

Design response

Add role-aware performance views that turn uploads into visible progress.

Outcome: motivation to practice
Anatomy of the Redesign

Video, transcript, and timeline remain connected.

The redesigned layout keeps the simulation as the visual anchor while the right panel provides focused analysis. Search and timeline tools shorten the path from a question to the exact evidence that answers it.

Design decision 01

Reorganize performance around clinical categories

Instead of making achievement status the main structure, the redesign groups criteria under the clinical category they belong to. Passed, incomplete, and missing states become attributes inside a more meaningful hierarchy.

✓One place for every category
✓Expandable depth when needed
✓Faster comparison within a clinical area
Category-based hierarchy
8 matching results
Design decision 02

Make the transcript searchable and contextual

The transcript now lives beside the video and supports search. Matching terms are highlighted, speaker colors stay consistent, and timestamps make it easier to connect language with the simulation.

✓Search by speaker or phrase
✓Highlighted results and result count
✓Video remains visible during review
Design decision 03

Offer two ways to understand time

The visual timeline provides a quick map of the simulation, while the list view gives users a precise sequence of evaluated events. Both use the same category colors as the report.

✓Visual scan for timing patterns
✓List view for exact evaluated moments
✓Consistent cross-interface color system
Timeline and list modes
Evidence in context
Design decision 04

Connect feedback to supporting evidence

Individual criteria expand to show written feedback, transcript evidence, and the relevant video moment. Users can understand not only the result, but also why the result was given.

✓Feedback, evidence, and playback together
✓Progressive disclosure limits cognitive load
✓Clearer foundation for trust in AI analysis
Design decision 05

Turn a score into an understandable story

The score breakdown combines the overall result, a category visualization, AI-generated summary, and exact category performance in one focused view. Users can move from a number to practical priorities.

1Category visualizationThe radar chart makes uneven performance visible across clinical areas without requiring users to compare rows one by one.
2Overall resultThe percentage and criteria count provide an immediate anchor before users move into the deeper explanation.
3AI-generated summaryStrengths, gaps, and priority areas are translated into a concise narrative that explains what the score means.
4Category-level detailExact counts, percentages, and progress bars let users verify the summary and identify where to improve next.

Beyond the Core Redesign

I also built the Performance Overview from scratch.

During the same internship, I designed a role-aware Performance Overview for understanding patterns across simulations. The goal was not only to display analytics, but to encourage continued uploads by making progress useful to students and engagement visible to administrators.

Direction-setting prototype
A collaborative foundation, still open to refinement

I worked with the team to establish the overall information architecture, visual language, and role-based direction. These screens document the agreed handoff direction for development. The implementation remains under construction and has room for testing and refinement after handoff.

Admin · 3 destinations

Cohort health and engagement

Administrators see Performance Overview, Detailed Scenario Matrix, and Student Roster. The overview answers whether the cohort is practicing, improving, and staying engaged.

Top-level engagementTotal uploads, scenarios practiced, and weekly engagement expose participation quickly.
Cohort patternsUpload volume, pass rate, performance trend, and leaderboard reveal change over time.
Student · 2 destinations

Personal progress and motivation

Students see Performance Overview and Detailed Scenario Matrix, but not Student Roster. The experience stays focused on their own practice, improvement, and next action.

Practice momentumUploads, scenarios, weekly engagement, and a practice streak encourage consistent participation.
Progress in contextPass rate, personal trend, and peer benchmarking make improvement easier to understand.
Shared drill-down

From averages to specific events

The matrix moves from scenario-level averages into categories, then exposes the top three best events and top three missed events. Users get a balanced explanation of what worked and what deserves attention.

Progressive disclosureCollapsed rows keep the matrix scannable while expanded categories reveal meaningful depth.
Actionable balanceBest events reinforce successful behavior while missed events create a focused improvement plan.
Admin only

Know who needs support

The roster adds the third admin destination. Uploads, scenarios, scores, weekly activity, streak, and status help administrators distinguish strong engagement from students who may be at risk.

Individual drill-downAdministrators can move from cohort patterns to the student behind the data.
Timely interventionStatus and activity signals make it easier to support learners before disengagement grows.

Outcome and Reflection
The goal was not to remove clinical complexity. It was to organize that complexity so users could understand the right information at the right moment.
Information continuityKeep context visible while users move into deeper detail.
Evidence builds trustAI feedback is more useful when users can verify where it came from.
